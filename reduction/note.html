A 38-point counterexample to interval rank-unimodality in the permutation pattern poset

McNamara and Steingrímsson conjectured that every interval in the classical permutation pattern poset is rank-unimodal [1, Conjecture 9.4]. In this order, α ≤ β when some subsequence of β has the relative order of α. Each distinct permutation in [σ, τ] counts once, at rank |π| − |σ|.

Theorem. Write P(w) for the permutation with increasing runs of lengths given by the positive word w, each run lying above every later run. Put

u = (17, 2, 16),   v = (2, (2, 1)7),

where powers denote repetition. Set σ = P(u) and τ = P(u) ⊕ P(v). Here the direct sum places its second summand to the right of and above its first. The endpoint lengths are 15 and 38. The sizes of ranks 14, 15 and 16 are respectively 3,662, 3,659 and 3,685. In particular the interval is not rank-unimodal.

Splitting at the direct sum

Every pattern in P(w) has the form P(a) for a unique word a. Indeed, the retained entries from one run increase, and the transition to the next retained run is a descent. Thus a embeds precisely when it can be matched, in order, to distinct slots of w whose capacities dominate its parts. Let Fw(x) count all such words by total weight, including the empty word.

Any pattern of length 29, 30 or 31 in the top has the form α ⊕ β using both sides. Its left part uses at least 6 entries, its right part at least 14. Since all runs have capacity at most 2, both parts use at least two runs. Each part is directly indecomposable: its first entry exceeds its last, whereas a nontrivial direct sum has first entry smaller than its last. The cut is therefore unique. Indeed, another cut would split one part as a nontrivial direct sum.

The bottom is directly indecomposable too, so every occurrence lies entirely on one side. On the left this requires α = σ. On the right let R(x) count the patterns containing σ by their length minus 15. With α = σ the right side is unrestricted. With α proper the right side must contain σ. Hence, for r = 14, 15, 16, the rank size is

[xr] ( Fv + (Fu − x15)R ).

Writing dk = 1 + x + ⋯ + xk, we have Fu = d14 + x2d7d6. A word either consists of units or has one double flanked by at most seven and six units, respectively.

The two right-side polynomials

In a greedy embedding each part uses the first remaining slot large enough to hold it. This succeeds whenever any embedding exists, since moving a match earlier preserves later choices. Put p = x + x2 and q = 2x2 + x3. Prepending a slot 2 to a host with polynomial f gives A(f) = 1 + pf. Prepending the slots (1, 2) gives B(f) = 1 + x + qf: a word that reaches the second slot starts with (2), (1, 1) or (1, 2), with arbitrary continuation. Since v = (2, 2, (1, 2)6, 1), we obtain Fv = A2(B6(1 + x)), with powers denoting composition. The identities 1 − (1 − p)A(f) = p[1 − (1 − p)f] and 1 − (1 − p)B(f) = q[1 − (1 − p)f] yield

(1 − x − x2)Fv = 1 − p2q7 = 1 − x16(1 + x)2(2 + x)7.

For R, the marked double of u needs seven earlier and six later slots in the 15-slot host. It can only occupy slot 8 or 9, and slot 9 has capacity 1. Consequently a right pattern containing σ uses every one of the first eight slots, with two entries from slot 8. Conversely this prefix suffices whenever the suffix uses at least six slots. The first seven slots offer four optional extra entries, contributing (1 + x)4.

The suffix has capacities (1, 2, 1, 2, 1, 2, 1). Relative to six mandatory units, using all seven slots contributes x(1 + x)3. With six slots, choose k of the three source doubles to retain as doubles. A retained double from position 2j appears at 2j or 2j − 1 in the output, identifying its source uniquely. Those before the omitted slot keep their positions and those after it shift left. Each of the k + 1 cuts is attainable by omitting a singleton before, between or after the retained doubles. Summing over the subsets gives the six-slot polynomial 1 + 6x + 9x2 + 4x3 = (1 + x)2(1 + 4x). Combining the choices gives

R = (1 + x)6(1 + 5x + x2).
